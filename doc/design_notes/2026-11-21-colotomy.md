# Design note: colotomy

Status: discussion (2026-11-21). Nothing here is implemented or decided.
Syntax updated to `:key value` options and `(part INSTRUMENT ...)`;
the current description of the whole language is
`2026-11-21-notation-language.md`.

This note covers issue 3 of the three notation issues (see
`2026-11-21-note-durations.md`) and builds on that note's durations,
`|` pulse checks and implied kempli. Musical facts are from Gold (2005),
summarised in `2026-11-21-reference-gold-2005.md`.

## Considerations

### Why a colotomy is needed

A colotomy (Gold's "colotomic meter") is a cycle with a fixed number of
pulses, marked by a pattern on gongs and the kempli. It gives a way to
say where something is: "cycle 4, pulse 3", as one would say "bar 4,
beat 3" in Western music. We need this for error messages and checks
now, and later to show cycle and pulse numbers in the score. Display is
not part of this note.

A colotomy can change within a piece. For now we assume it is fixed.

### What a colotomy is, following Gold

- The gong marks the end of a cycle, which is also the start of the
  next. Pulses are counted 1 to N with the gong on N.
- Each meter has a name (gilak, bapang, batel ...) that travels with the
  pattern. So a colotomy is a named, reusable definition, like a scale or
  an instrument, and belongs in templates.
- The gong patterns are fixed, so pieces should not have to write them,
  in the same way the kempli is not written.
- The timekeeper is usually the kempli, but in some meters (batel,
  omang) it is the kajar.
- Some meters have strokes between pulses (the kelenang on the
  offbeats), so colotomic patterns need the duration notation.

### A piece that opens on the gong

Pieces and examples often begin on a gong stroke. With the gong on
pulse N, that opening stroke is the last pulse of cycle 0, and the piece
starts with a pickup (LilyPond's `\partial`). This removes the problem
in `examples/tenzer1.nts`, where the closing gong had to be numbered
pulse 9.

### Colotomy is optional

A piece can be written without a colotomy: excerpts and exercises often
have none. Then positions are counted in pulses only. The kempli is
still implied, one stroke per pulse, with or without a colotomy.

## The notation so far

### Defining a colotomy

```scheme
(colotomy NAME LENGTH :key value ... (PART INSTRUMENT TOKEN ...) ...)
```

- LENGTH is the cycle length in pulses.
- Each `(PART INSTRUMENT TOKEN ...)` is a colotomic part over one cycle,
  written with ordinary part tokens, durations included. A part shorter
  than LENGTH repeats to fill the cycle; its length must divide LENGTH.
  Pulse k of the cycle starts at time k − 1, so the gong on pulse N
  starts at N − 1 and the cycle ends at N.
- Options:
  - `:timekeeper INSTRUMENT`: the instrument of the implied pulse,
    one stroke per pulse. Default `kempli`.
  - `:lines N`: the cycle is written in lines of N pulses (optional;
    see open questions).
- A colotomy that defines no timekeeper part still gets the implied
  one: one stroke per pulse. A timekeeper part is only written when it
  does something else.

### Using a colotomy

```scheme
(piece TITLE :colotomy NAME :pickup P ...)
```

- `:colotomy NAME` chooses the colotomy. The reader generates its parts
  for the length of the piece, as ordinary parts.
- `:pickup P`: the piece starts P pulses before the end of a cycle.
  Default 0. It is an error without a colotomy.
- Parts written in the piece can still include gong parts, for music
  that departs from the colotomy.

### Positions and checks

- Inside the program, time stays an integer step count (duration note).
  The colotomy and pickup convert a time to a position and back:
  piece pulse 13 is cycle 2, pulse 4, with a one-pulse pickup in an
  8-pulse cycle.
- Errors give positions as cycle and pulse ("part `ugal`, cycle 2,
  pulse 4 1/2"), or pulses only without a colotomy.
- `|` checks a pulse boundary. `||` checks a cycle boundary: the time
  so far, counted from the start of the piece including the pickup,
  must be a whole number of cycles. `||` without a colotomy is an
  error. Guile's default reader reads `||` as an ordinary symbol, as it
  does `|` (tested with Guile 3.0.9).
- Forms that need a position (later: silencing the kempli, sections)
  write it as a list, `(3 1)` for cycle 3, pulse 1. `3.1` is not
  possible: Guile reads it as a decimal number.

### Prepared for change, built for one

The piece holds its colotomy as a list of segments, each a start
position and a colotomy. For now the list always has one segment.
Changing colotomies by section would add segments without changing the
record.

## A complete example

New definitions for `templates/gong-kebyar.nts`. The stroke symbols
follow Gold (G, P, T, n), with `GL` added for the gong lanang:

```scheme
(strokes gong-strokes
  G                ; gong wadon (lower)
  GL               ; gong lanang (higher)
  P                ; kempur
  T                ; klentong (kemong)
  n)               ; kelenang
(strokes kajar-strokes  K)

(instrument gong   gong-strokes   G GL P T n)
(instrument kajar  kajar-strokes  K)

;; Gold fig. 5.11. Pulse k starts at time k - 1; the gong is on the
;; last pulse.

;; gilak: gong lanang on 4, kempur on 5 and 7, gong wadon on 8
(colotomy gilak 8
  (gong gong  -:3 GL:1 P - P G))

;; bapang: kempur on 2 and 6, klentong on 4, gong on 8
(colotomy bapang 8
  (gong gong  - P - T - P - G))

;; gegaboran: kempur on 4 and 12, klentong on 8, gong on 16
(colotomy gegaboran 16
  (gong gong  -:3 P:1 -:3 T:1 -:3 P:1 -:3 G:1))

;; batel: klentong on 1, kempur on 2, kelenang on the offbeats;
;; the kajar keeps the pulse
(colotomy batel 2 :timekeeper kajar
  (gong gong  T:1/2 n P n))
```

`examples/tenzer1.nts`, which turns out to be gilak:

```scheme
;; Eight-pulse gong cycle (gilak) with ugal melody.
;; The piece opens on the gong: a one-pulse pickup, then one cycle.
;; The gong, kempur and kempli parts come from the colotomy.

(piece "Eight-pulse gong cycle" :colotomy gilak :pickup 1)

(use gong-kebyar)

;;               pickup  cycle 1: pulses 1-8, gong on 8
(part ugal       a1 ||   o2 i2 e2 o2 | a1 o2 i2 a1 ||)

(group "Ugal" ugal)
```

The generated gong part puts the gong wadon on the pickup (cycle 0,
pulse 8), the gong lanang on pulse 4, the kempur on 5 and 7, and the
gong wadon on 8. This is the pattern of the original figure.

## Open questions

1. **Gong strokes.** Are `G`, `GL`, `P`, `T`, `n` the right names? Are
   gong, kempur, klentong and kelenang one instrument with several
   strokes (as above) or separate instruments?
2. **Rests in gong parts.** Gongs are never damped, so `-` (rest, damped)
   is wrong for them. This depends on open question 2 in
   `01-notation.md`, which Gold's cipher dot answers as "nothing new".
3. **Lines.** Gold writes long cycles in lines of 8 pulses. Should a
   position have a line level (cycle, line, pulse)? Is there a check for
   a line boundary?
4. **Departing from the colotomy.** How does a piece change or add to
   a generated part (an extra kempur stroke, a kempli that stops)?
   Replace the whole generated part, or patch it at positions?
5. **Incomplete cycles.** Can a piece end mid-cycle, or must it be a
   pickup plus whole cycles?
6. **Longgor forms.** Should a doubled-length meter be defined from its
   short form? Gold's figure suggests only the gongs are stretched while
   the kelenang offbeats stay, so it is not a simple `(* 2 ...)`.
7. **The timekeeper's pattern.** In some pieces the kajar plays drum
   patterns rather than even pulses (Gold p. 134). Is that a timekeeper
   part, or an ordinary part?
8. **Pickup limits.** Can the pickup be longer than a cycle, or a
   fraction of a pulse?
9. **Checking the meters.** The definitions above follow figure 5.11.
   Omang and gabor longgor are left out until their kelenang positions
   are checked against the recording or another source.
10. **Beyond a fixed colotomy.** Changing meters between sections,
    irregular cycles, pulse doubling and metrically free passages are
    known limits (see the reference note).
